Where in the race

Dots mark where each of the 16 stretches starts. The highlighted stretches are the two keyframes this frame is blended from; the stronger one counts more. Dashed: the straight line the nearest stretch is measured against.

The frame at this position

One cycle (2048 samples in the wavetable), drawn twice: the faded copy is the next repeat. Its end meets its start, so it loops without a click.

Ingredients of the nearest keyframe

A. Bend: metres off the straight line (+ right, − left)

B. Heading: degrees compared with a steady turn (+ left, − right)

C. Hills: elevation in metres; the shaded gap from the steady slope is what counts

The original recipe: longitude, elevation, latitude

The first way I made these wavetables: take one reading from the whole run, start to finish, and squeeze it into a single cycle. Longitude is how far east or west you were, elevation how high, latitude how far north or south. The morph wavetable blends longitude into elevation into latitude across its 256 frames. These waves end at a different level from where they start, which gives them a buzzy edge when they loop; progress mode avoids that by measuring each stretch against a straight line from its start to its end.

Each run is cut into 16 overlapping stretches of road. A keyframe mixes three ingredients from its stretch: how far the road bends from a straight line, how your direction changes compared with a steady turn (every corner adds brightness), and how the hills rise and fall compared with a steady slope. Between each pair of keyframes, 16 frames blend gradually from one to the next, so sweeping the wavetable position doesn’t click.

Some keyframes are stored upside down so they match their neighbour. A held note sounds the same either way. The sound here plays a 512-sample version of each frame, so it previews the tone rather than matching a synth exactly.

To use your own run, export a GPX file: on Strava, open the activity on the website, click the … menu and choose Export GPX; on Garmin Connect, use the gear menu on the activity. Your file never leaves your browser: the page does all the maths on your device, and the map of your route isn’t sent anywhere. The downloads are full 256-frame, 2048-sample wavetables that load in Serum and Vital.

In Chrome or Edge you can link a folder instead of downloading. It’s the same folder Spice Drawer and Chord Pantry use, and each tool saves into its own subfolder, so your wavetables land in Waverunner next to your samples and MIDI. Once it’s linked, every GPX you add saves both wavetables there straight away.

These are four of my races, tracked on my watch: the Cherry Blossom 10 Miler and the Eugene, Marine Corps and Philly marathons. Each one becomes a 256-frame wavetable for Serum and Vital, and so can yours.
